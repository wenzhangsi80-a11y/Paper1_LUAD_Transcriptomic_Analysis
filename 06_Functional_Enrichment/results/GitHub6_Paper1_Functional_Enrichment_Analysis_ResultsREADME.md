# Functional Enrichment Analysis — Results

## 1. Overview

This directory contains the results, quality-control records, provenance information, and reproducibility audits generated by the two functional enrichment analysis scripts:

- `GitHub6_Paper1_Functional_Enrichment_Analysis_1.R`
- `GitHub6_Paper1_Functional_Enrichment_Analysis_2.R`

The workflows support the study entitled **“Integrated transcriptomic and single-cell characterization of AOC3, MS4A7, and IL7R in LUAD.”**

The two scripts implement complementary but distinct analytical components:

| Script | Main analytical scope |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| `GitHub6_Paper1_Functional_Enrichment_Analysis_1.R` | Upstream input inventory and QC, revised DEG definition and locking, GO/KEGG over-representation analysis (ORA), GO–KEGG evidence integration, semantic redundancy analysis, and preparation of the genome-wide GSEA input audit |
| `GitHub6_Paper1_Functional_Enrichment_Analysis_2.R` | Genome-wide preranked GSEA input preparation and locking, KEGG and GO-BP GSEA, result and provenance audits, and descriptive comparison with historical Reactome pathway observations |

The workflows distinguish DEG-based ORA from genome-wide preranked GSEA. They also distinguish enrichment calculations from subsequent structural audits, evidence integration, metadata repairs, and final result-lock procedures.

The outputs are organized to preserve the relationship between source data, analysis inputs, statistical results, intermediate evidence tables, and final audit records.

## 2. Workflow Organization

The principal workflow sequence is:

1.  **STEP9A — Upstream input inventory and QC**
2.  **STEP9B-6D and STEP9B-7R — Historical DEG definition recovery and revised DEG lock**
3.  **STEP9C — GO/KEGG ORA, identifier mapping, and result locking**
4.  **STEP9C-7A to STEP9C-7D — Semantic redundancy analysis and GO–KEGG evidence integration**
5.  **STEP9D-1 — Genome-wide GSEA input discovery and source audit**
6.  **STEP9D-1H/1I and subsequent STEP9D input-lock operations — Ranking preparation, identifier mapping, and input integrity checks**
7.  **STEP9D-3 — Formal KEGG preranked GSEA**
8.  **STEP9D-4 — Formal GO-BP preranked GSEA**
9.  **STEP9D-4C — GO-BP GSEA result and reproducibility audit**
10. **STEP9D-5, STEP9D-5B, and STEP9D-5C — Historical Reactome theme comparison, strict evidence auditing, and final evidence review/locking**

The detailed STEP identifiers and output filenames are retained in the corresponding workflow directories. The sections below summarize the purpose, analytical definitions, important reference values, and principal output types.

### 4.2 STEP9B-7R: Revised DEG Analysis

STEP9B-7R establishes the revised DEG testing universe and generates the revised tumor-versus-normal differential expression results.

The revised analysis uses the expression data and sample annotations defined by the upstream workflow.

The principal analysis definitions are:

| Component | Definition |
|----|----|
| Expression scale | `log2(FPKM-UQ + 1)` |
| Comparison | Tumor versus Normal |
| Statistical test | Wilcoxon rank-sum test |
| Effect size | Mean Tumor expression minus mean Normal expression |
| Multiple-testing correction | Benjamini–Hochberg |
| Significance threshold | BH FDR \< 0.05 |
| Effect-size threshold | `|log2FC| ≥ 1` |

The effect size is calculated from the transformed expression values. It is not a ratio calculated directly from raw FPKM-UQ values.

The revised DEG testing universe contains 57,828 genes. This universe is distinct from the 31,609-gene tumor-only WGCNA universe.

## 6. STEP9C-7 — Semantic Redundancy and GO–KEGG Evidence Integration

### 6.1 GO-BP semantic similarity and redundancy

STEP9C-7A evaluates semantic similarity among significant GO-BP terms and generates descriptive redundancy summaries.

The workflow uses:

- Wang semantic similarity.
- Complete-linkage hierarchical clustering.
- Similarity thresholds of 0.50, 0.60, 0.70, and 0.80.

The 0.70 threshold is the primary descriptive threshold used for representative-term organization.

The threshold is not a statistical significance threshold. Representative terms are used to organize and visualize redundant GO-BP results; the full set of significant enrichment results is retained.

Outputs include semantic-similarity RDS objects, clustering records, redundancy summaries, cluster membership tables at the specified thresholds, representative-term tables, and audit records.

The file-location diagnostic associated with this workflow is distinct from the semantic similarity calculation. Where the diagnostic only reports file locations or object availability in the console, it should not be interpreted as generating an additional result table.

### 6.2 GO–KEGG integration

STEP9C-7B integrates the locked GO-BP and KEGG ORA results.

The integration includes:

- All GO–KEGG term/pathway pairs.
- Nonzero-overlap pairs.
- Cross-direction overlap pairs.
- Direction-overlap audits.
- Direct study-gene overlap audits.
- Integration summaries.
- Final lock records.

The integration is descriptive and is derived from previously locked enrichment results. It does not perform a new enrichment calculation or modify the original GO-BP or KEGG result files.

STEP9C-7B-READ provides a read-only evidence audit of the integration, including overall overlap summaries, connectivity, overlap distributions, evidence-pair tables, and study-gene summaries.

### 7.3 Ranking preparation and identifier mapping

The subsequent STEP9D input-preparation workflow establishes the ranking vectors and database-specific gene identifiers used for formal GSEA.

- Source-file and ranking-structure checks.

- GO-BP annotation and input-policy locking.
- Ranking tie metadata and numerical-integrity checks.

The relevant STEP9D input-lock files and mapping audit outputs are retained in their corresponding STEP9D subdirectories.

## 10. STEP9D-5B — Strict Theme Evidence Audit

### 10.1 Purpose

STEP9D-5B provides a more detailed thematic evidence inventory than the preceding STEP9D-5 concordance summary.

It compares:

1.  Historical Xiantao Reactome reference themes.
2.  Locked genome-wide KEGG GSEA results from STEP9D-3B.
3.  Locked genome-wide GO-BP GSEA results from STEP9D-4B.

Unlike STEP9D-5, STEP9D-5B does not assign Strong, Moderate, Limited, or No concordance classifications.

### 10.2 Direct and contextual keyword dictionaries

The workflow defines separate direct and contextual keyword dictionaries for IL7R, MS4A7, and AOC3.

- **Direct terms** represent relatively specific terminology associated with the corresponding historical Reactome theme.
- **Contextual terms** represent related terminology that is not sufficient by itself to establish a direct thematic match.

The primary matching operation uses the pathway `Description` field only.

The `leading_edge` and `core_enrichment` fields are retained as supporting information but are not used to determine the theme-match classification.

### 10.3 Evidence quadrants

Each pathway is assigned to one of five descriptive evidence categories:

| Evidence category            | Definition                                 |
|------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| `Direct_significant`         | Direct theme match with FDR \< 0.05        |
| `Contextual_significant`     | Contextual theme match with FDR \< 0.05    |
| `Direct_non_significant`     | Direct theme match without FDR \< 0.05     |
| `Contextual_non_significant` | Contextual theme match without FDR \< 0.05 |
| `No_theme_match`             | Neither direct nor contextual theme match  |

The workflow also produces gene- and database-specific descriptive summaries.

The classification depends on predefined keyword terminology and pathway-description wording. A direct text match does not establish identical biological mechanisms across databases, while a contextual match should not be interpreted as direct pathway equivalence.

| Descriptive integration | Semantic redundancy, GO–KEGG overlap, and Reactome theme evidence summaries |

A result audit is not an independent reproduction of the analysis that generated the source result.

## 13. Interpretation and Limitations

The functional enrichment workflows provide statistical and descriptive evidence for biological processes and pathways associated with the revised LUAD transcriptomic analysis.

The results should be interpreted within the following limitations:

1.  ORA and preranked GSEA use different input definitions and should be interpreted separately.
2.  GO–KEGG overlap and semantic similarity are descriptive evidence-integration procedures, not additional independent enrichment tests.
3.  Semantic similarity thresholds organize redundant GO-BP terms; they are not statistical significance thresholds.
4.  Direct and contextual Reactome theme matches depend on predefined keyword dictionaries and pathway-description terminology.
5.  Historical Reactome observations are retained as reference information from the original Xiantao analysis. Their comparison with the revised KEGG and GO-BP results is not independent external validation.
6.  Pathway-level correspondence does not establish identical biological mechanisms, direct molecular interactions, or causal relationships.
7.  Bulk-tissue enrichment results may reflect differences in tissue and cell-type composition as well as cell-intrinsic expression patterns.
8.  Direct study-gene overlap with a pathway or enrichment result does not by itself establish leading-edge status, pathway activity, or causality.
9.  These analyses do not establish clinical diagnostic performance or clinical biomarker validity.

The three study genes, AOC3, MS4A7, and IL7R, are treated as prioritized study genes for downstream characterization. The functional enrichment results should not be used to imply that all three genes were formally ranked in the initial candidate-selection procedure or that all three are PPI hubs.
